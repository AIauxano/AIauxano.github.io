Third-Party Risk: Tiering, SOC 2 Review and AI Vendor Due Diligence

Tiered 12 vendors by data and criticality, reviewed a card processor's SOC 2 report, and ran focused due diligence on an LLM vendor with no audit report.

Artifacts

The situation

Procurement checked price only. A 60-person outsourced support team and an AI vendor were handling payee bank details with no assessment on file.

Approach

  1. Scored data sensitivity, access and criticality; derived tier and required due diligence.
  2. Read the card processor's SOC 2: period, opinion, exceptions, carve-outs, complementary user entity controls.
  3. Built a 15-20 question questionnaire for the LLM vendor instead of a 300-question form, then listed required contract clauses.

Analysis

VendorTierWhy

Northlight AITier 1Full ticket text despite $22k spend

PromoCrateTier 3Office addresses only

Findings

  • Spend is a poor proxy for risk.
  • The SOC 2 period ended September 30: a bridge letter is needed.
  • "Approvals obtained verbally" is a design weakness, not a fix.
  • One CUEC maps to Larkspur's own failed offboarding control.

Recommendations

  • Accept Tessera with follow-ups and a 90-day check-in.
  • Require no-training, 30-day deletion, 48-hour breach notice and subprocessor notice from the AI vendor.

Context: Larkspur Pay is a fictional company in the Hands-On GRC Training Program. All names and data are invented.
