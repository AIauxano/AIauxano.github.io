Risk Register and FAIR Quantification

Built a scored risk register from interviews and scan data, then used a 10,000-year Monte Carlo to show a $180k control spend avoids about $370k of expected annual loss.

NIST SP 800-30ISO/IEC 27005FAIR

Artifacts

The situation

No risk register existed. The CFO asked whether $180k a year for immutable backups and better EDR was worth it against ransomware that could stop payouts for two days or more.

Approach

  1. Set 5-point likelihood and impact scales sized to $96M revenue, plus an acceptance-authority table.
  2. Turned interview notes and data pulls into risk statements: cause, event, business impact.
  3. Scored inherent and residual risk and assigned business owners.
  4. Ran a FAIR-style Monte Carlo with PERT distributions for threat frequency, vulnerability and loss.
  5. Wrote a 90-day risk acceptance for a risk engineering would not fix this quarter.

Findings

  • Expected loss falls about $370k a year for $180k: roughly $2 avoided per $1 spent.
  • Bad-year exposure falls about $2.7M, which is the stronger argument for a CFO.
  • The vulnerability estimate drives the answer most; it is stated as the main caveat.

Recommendations

  • Fund the backup and EDR work and test restores quarterly.
  • Accept the GitHub bypass risk for 90 days only, with bypass alerts and owner count cut from nine to three.
