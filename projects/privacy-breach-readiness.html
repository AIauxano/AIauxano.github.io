Privacy Compliance: ROPA, DPIA and Breach-Notification Clock

Mapped a support data flow across three countries, ran a DPIA on an AI tool already in production, and showed the binding breach deadline was DORA's four hours, not GDPR's 72.

GDPR Arts. 30, 33-35DORA incident reportingUS state breach laws

Artifacts

  • Lab workbook (T_Obligations, T_ROPA, T_DPIA, T_BreachClock)

The situation

Support tickets with EU bank details flowed to agents in the Philippines and an LLM API in the US. Then a scenario breach exposed 18,400 tickets.

Approach

  1. Turned GDPR, DORA and PCI text into testable requirements tied to controls and owners.
  2. Mapped the flow and wrote the Article 30 record, including transfers and safeguards.
  3. Scored DPIA risks before and after measures and made a recommendation for a live tool.
  4. Built a notification matrix across every regime triggered.

Analysis

RegimeDeadline (Eastern)
DORA initial noticeMon 18:00 (4h after classification)
Customer contractsWed 10:30 (48h)
GDPR Art. 33Thu 10:30 (72h)
US statesEarliest 30 days

Findings

  • Two international transfers needed safeguards (Philippines, US).
  • Bank details in free-text tickets fail data minimization; redaction at intake fixes most of it.
  • Teams that only know GDPR miss the DORA deadline.

Recommendations

  • Pause sending bank-detail tickets to the AI tool until redaction and zero-retention are in place.
  • Name who classifies and who notifies in the first four hours.

Context: Larkspur Pay is a fictional company in the Hands-On GRC Training Program. All names and data are invented.
