Regulatory Applicability and Control Crosswalk

Worked out which laws, contracts and frameworks bind a US/EU payments company, then mapped one control set across four frameworks so each control is tested once and reused.

PCI DSS v4.0.1GDPRDORASOC 2ISO/IEC 27001:2022NIST CSF 2.0

The situation

Larkspur Pay runs a payouts platform from North Carolina and an e-money entity in Dublin. It stores card numbers, pays 2.3M people (18% in the EU) and sells to enterprises that demand a SOC 2 report. The new CISO asked for one page: what is mandatory, what is market-driven, and what needs Legal.

Approach

  1. Listed every obligation source: laws, regulators, card-brand contracts, customer contracts and certifications customers ask for.
  2. For each one recorded the trigger fact at Larkspur, the proof required, cadence and owner.
  3. Flagged items that turn on legal interpretation instead of guessing.
  4. Mapped nine core controls to ISO 27001 Annex A, SOC 2 TSC, PCI DSS and CSF 2.0 using NIST CPRT, then checked each mapping against the standard's own text.

Findings

  • Four regimes are binding today; SOC 2 is optional in law but mandatory in practice.
  • Three items need Legal: FTC Safeguards Rule status, CCPA's GLBA carve-outs, and Ireland's NIS2 transposition.
  • Mappings are many-to-many and loose: PCI 7.2.4 sets a six-month review floor while the company standard says quarterly.

Recommendations

  • Use NIST CSF 2.0 for board reporting and a single common control set mapped to PCI DSS and SOC 2.
  • Adopt the rule that the strictest requirement in scope sets the control.
